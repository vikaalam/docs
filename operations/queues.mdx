## What a queue is

A **queue** is one team's shared pool of work. Tasks land in a queue first and are then claimed by,
or assigned to, a person.

It is also the primary **permission boundary**. A compliance reviewer may read and act inside
`queue:compliance` and not inside `queue:treasury` — enforced on the server, on every request.

<Note>
  **A hidden button is not a permission.** The interface mirrors your permissions so navigation makes
  sense, but the server is the only enforcement point. Nothing you cannot see is protected only by
  not being rendered.
</Note>

  answered without a SQL query against production. This screen is that query, run continuously.
</Tip>

## Queues are not seniority levels

This is the distinction most often got wrong when queues are first configured.

Two teams reviewing the same subject for **different reasons** are two queues, not two tiers of one.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The worked example: an unattributable deposit" icon="money-bill-transfer">
    Money arrives and the automatic attribution cannot decide whose it is. Two very different reviews
    can follow:

    - **Name review** → belongs to **operations**. Is this counterparty who we think it is?
    - **Threshold review** → belongs to **compliance**. This amount crosses a reporting threshold.

    They are not seniority levels of one queue. They are **different work, with different deadlines**,
    and a person in one is usually not in the other.

    Modelling them as one queue with an escalation level produces work in front of people who cannot
    action it, and an SLA that means two different things depending on the row.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Escalation across two queues, without duplicate notification

When work escalates from one team to another, both tiers keep the thread and **neither gets told
twice**.

This works because everything caused by one original fact carries the same **correlation id**. The
second escalation is a new task with a new owner and a new deadline, and it is still part of the same
story — so the case view shows one thread, not two disconnected items.

## Changing who gets what

Responsibility rules are **configuration**, edited in the interface, versioned, and audited with a
before/after diff.

Changing who is responsible for a kind of work does not require a deploy and does not require an
engineer. It does produce an audit entry with your name on it — because changing who is notified
about a compliance failure is exactly as consequential as a code change.

<Warning>
  A responsibility-rule change does **not** retroactively change who can see historical work.
  Authorization scope is derived once, when the work is created, precisely so that a rule change
  cannot silently alter the visibility of the past.
</Warning>
