## The four regions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Outcome form" icon="clipboard-list">
    The decision, as structured fields.

    Configured per **task type**, so a sanctions-screening review and a payout-destination approval
    ask different questions. Adding a new kind of human work is a configuration change, not a deploy.

    **Why structured and not free text:** your answer feeds a case, a report, or both. A process step
    cannot branch on a paragraph, and a regulatory artifact cannot cite one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence" icon="paperclip">
    Documents the case gathered automatically — a fetched identity document, a screening result — plus
    anything you or a colleague attached.

    Attachments are held by a platform-level artifact store, which is also where retention and legal
    hold apply to them. They are not a folder in a chat channel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provided links" icon="link">
    Links that arrived **with the original event**, shown with their **provenance**.

    This is a deliberate design decision, not a detail. A link inside an envelope is the one URL a
    reviewer is trained to click, so the interface always states where it came from rather than
    presenting it as if the platform vouched for it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activity" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    Assignment, reassignment, comments, and every state change — with who and when.

    This is the *operational* history, held on the task for fast reading. It is separate from the
    permanent audit record, which is kept for years and cannot be edited by anyone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What is always on the row

| Field | Why it is there |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject** | What this task is about — a deposit, a customer, a destination. Shown as a type and a reference |
| **Reason** | Why this task exists, with the values that triggered it |
| **Due** | The deadline, and how close it is |
| **Queue** | Which team pool it belongs to — also the permission boundary |
| **Priority** | Ordinal, and encoded as an ordinal rather than a colour, because priority is a rank and colour is a category |

## Four eyes, when it applies

Some tasks carry a **four-eyes** control: the person who acted earlier in the chain may not be the
person who approves now.

This is enforced by the process engine, not by policy or by asking people to be careful. If you were
the maker, you will not be offered the approval — and an attempt to take it is **recorded as a
rejected action**, not silently ignored.

<Warning>
  **The hardest maker-checker failure is not a missed approval — it is an approval chain where one
  person played two roles and it looked complete.**

  That is why the exclusion is enforced by the engine and why the attempt is recorded. A control you
  can only verify by reading names is not a control.
</Warning>

## Comments

Comments live on the task, not in a chat thread. That is the difference between a discussion you can
find in a year and one you cannot.

<Note>
  Chat is still a **destination** — notifications can land there. What changes is that the *record*
  of the work lives with the work.
</Note>

## When a task is stuck on something else

Sometimes the honest answer is "I am waiting for a document" or "I need the counterparty to reply".

That is a state, not a blocked queue item: the task records what it is waiting on, the timer stays
armed, and if the wait exceeds its deadline the task escalates rather than sitting silent.

## What happens when you submit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your outcome is written">
    Together with the audit entry, **inside the same transaction**. If the record cannot be written,
    your change does not stand — deliberately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The task publishes an event">
    `task.item.state_changed`. It goes to the same pipeline every other event goes through.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If it belonged to a case, the case advances">
    Through a workflow whose action signals the process. The task itself never calls the case — which
    is exactly why an ad-hoc task with no case behaves identically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Anyone configured to be told, is told">
    Through the notification module, on the channels the policy specifies. If that resolves to
    nobody, it becomes a visible item — not a silent success.
  </Step>
</Steps>
