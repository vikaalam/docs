An ordinary day for a compliance or operations analyst. Every screen shown here is a real design
export.

## 09:00 — Open Attention

<img
  src="/images/screens/ops-attention.webp"
  alt="Ops · Attention — nine cards, scoped to operations"
  noZoom
/>

**Attention** is the first thing you open, and on most days the only thing you need before lunch.

It unions the work of six different parts of the platform into one list: tasks assigned to you, tasks
nobody has picked up, cases waiting past their deadline, events nobody has configured a reaction for,
notifications that reached nobody, and automated steps that gave up after retrying.

<Note>
  **Attention aggregates and owns nothing.** Every row belongs to a real screen somewhere else, and
  clicking it takes you there. That is deliberate — it means the screen can never grow logic of its
  own and start disagreeing with the thing it summarises.
</Note>

What you are reading, top to bottom:

| Signal | Means | Do what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Something broke, or a deadline has passed | Now |
| Amber | Something reached nobody, or nobody has claimed it | Today |
| Grey | Suppressed or duplicate — recorded, no action | Nothing |
| A count that is not falling | The queue is not draining | Tell your lead |

## 09:20 — Work your own list

<img
  src="/images/screens/ops-my-tasks.webp"
  alt="Ops · My tasks — grouped by the workflow that created each task"
  noZoom
/>

**My tasks** is your work and only yours, grouped by **the workflow that created each task** —
because the task type, its priority and its deadline are properties of that workflow's configuration,
true for the group and not for any single row.

Each row carries the four things you need before opening anything: **what it is**, **what it is
about** (the subject), **when it is due**, and **why it exists**.

That last one is the change from today. A task created because an automatic check declined to decide
says *which* check and *what value* — not "review needed".

<Tip>
  Prefer a board? The same data is available as a Kanban view, grouped by workflow state rather than
  listed. It is a view option, not a different screen — same tasks, same deadlines.
</Tip>

## 10:15 — Open one task

<img
  src="/images/screens/task-detail.webp"
  alt="Task detail — the outcome form, evidence, provided links, and the activity history"
  noZoom
/>

One unit of work, opened. Four regions:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The outcome form">
    What you are being asked to decide, as a form — not as free text. The fields are configured per
    task type, so a screening review and a payout approval ask different questions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence">
    Attachments and documents gathered by the case, plus anything you add.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provided links">
    Links that came in with the event, **with their provenance shown**. A link in an envelope is the
    one URL a reviewer is trained to click, so the interface always says where it came from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activity">
    Everything that has happened to this task: assignment, reassignment, comments, state changes, with
    who and when.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 11:40 — A case is stuck

<img
  src="/images/screens/ops-cases.webp"
  alt="Ops · Cases — where each case is, and what it is waiting on"
  noZoom
/>

A **case** is a long-running sequence — onboarding, an enhanced review, a filing. The list shows
which cases are running and what each is waiting on; opening one shows the ordered steps and their
kind, where it is right now, the armed timers, and the sub-tasks handed to people.

<Note>
  On the current design, **no status is drawn on any row** — because no case data carries one yet, and
  a neutral pill would read as `waiting`, which is a real status. The absence is rendered as an
  absence.
</Note>

<Note>
  **"Why is this stuck" is a field, not an investigation.** In the starting condition, answering it
  meant opening the monitoring tool, the case notes, the chat channel and the database.
</Note>

## 14:00 — Check the team queue

<img
  src="/images/screens/queues.webp"
  alt="Queues — depth, unassigned work, and SLA health per team"
  noZoom
/>

If you lead a team, this is your screen. Depth, unassigned work, and SLA health per queue.

A queue is also the main **permission scope**: a compliance reviewer can see and act inside
`queue:compliance` and not inside `queue:treasury`. That is not a UI filter — it is enforced on the
server, and a hidden button is never treated as a permission.

## 16:30 — "Did anyone actually get told?"

<img
  src="/images/screens/ops-notifications.webp"
  alt="Ops · Notifications — outcomes only, never settings"
  noZoom
/>

**Notifications** is the record of what reached a person. Every send, its recipient count, its
channel, its outcome.

Note there is no `delivered` — the outcomes stop at `handed_off`, because the notifier owns the last
mile and this platform does not claim to know what it cannot see.

The row that matters most is the one that reached nobody. It is marked `no_recipient` — **amber, not
red and not green**. Amber because the action completed and reached nobody: red would read as a
failed send, and green would be a lie.

<Warning>
  A `no_recipient` delivery makes its **event** `partial`, not `dispatched`. The two screens agree by
  construction. Before this rule, the event history showed green for an alert nobody received, and
  the finding lived only in the delivery log where nobody looked.
</Warning>

The usual cause is not a broken integration. It is a **hole in the responsibility rules** — nobody is
configured as responsible for that combination. That is a configuration fix, and the queue tells you
which one.

## 17:00 — Close the loop

Nothing to do. Every action you took today is already recorded with your name, the time, and what the
thing looked like before you touched it — not because you remembered to write it down, but because
the record is written inside the same transaction as the change.
