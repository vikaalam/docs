The provisioning half of the Operations remit, and the one screen that holds **both** kinds of row.

<Warning>
  **It cannot list a virtual account and cannot provision one.** Virtual accounts are created,
  approved, retried and closed in the source system.

  The banner says so **above the first row**, for a reason the design states plainly: *a table of
  `VA-` references will be read as a VA list within a week.*

  No create button. No row action. No deep link — **there is no address for the source system anywhere
  in this design.**
</Warning>

## Two cards, never merged

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Arrived">
    Events that actually reached the platform. **Two of twenty-six rows.**

    | Ref | What happened |
    | --- | --- |
    | `VA-1180` | Provisioning **failed**, and the reaction is `dead` — a vendor call exhausted 6 attempts on `502`, and its notification exhausted 5 on connection refused |
    | `VA-1182` | Provisioning **requested** — and `unmapped` |

    **Zero tasks, zero case signals, zero deliveries** across both. *That* is the finding.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Capability">
    What the source service **could** publish — five catalogue entries, with **readiness** columns
    instead of arrival columns.

    Occurred-at and event-id render **readiness words, never values**, because the catalogue names
    source columns and not timestamps.

    **Two of the five are registered. The other three answer `404` at the door** — which is
    deliberately *not* the same thing as `unmapped`.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  **Merging the two cards would make an unpublished event look like a quiet one.** A catalogue row is a
  possibility; an arrived row is an occurrence.
</Note>

## The screen's most operational fact

<Warning>
  **`VA-1182` — provisioning was requested, and nothing reacted.**

  The event arrived cleanly. It is registered. There is simply no workflow for it yet, so its status
  is `unmapped`.

  **Money-adjacent provisioning was requested and nothing happened.**
</Warning>

This is also a clean illustration of a boundary the platform draws deliberately:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The consequence** | Operational — it is your provisioning request that stalled |
| **The fix** | Engineering's — somebody has to build the workflow |

So it appears on **your** Attention screen as a fact, and is listed on the engineering side as the
row that needs a configuration change. Neither team can quietly assume the other saw it.

## `404` and `unmapped` are different answers

| Answer | Means | Who fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `404` | The event type **is not registered**. The door does not exist | A configurator registers it |
| `unmapped` | It **is** registered and arrived, and no workflow matches | A configurator builds the workflow |

Three of the five catalogue entries here are the first case. `VA-1182` is the second.

## Two catalogue contradictions, printed rather than resolved

<Warning>
  **The same pair of failures is filed under two different event-type names** in two places in the
  catalogue.

  The registry holds one of them. The other is **named in a callout and deliberately not rendered as a
  row** — because drawing it would merge the two corpora, which is exactly what the two-card split
  exists to prevent.
</Warning>

And the duplicated upstream message from [Parties](/operations/subjects/parties) turns up again here:
one message, *"Failed to add VA for partner"*, for two genuinely different failures.

## Deliberately absent

- **Every person's name** — no directory sample data
- **The party behind either VA**, beyond its opaque reference
- **Every VA attribute no publisher sent**
- **Whether either account now exists** — the platform does not know, and does not pretend to
