This is the operational face of the [unattributable-deposit
scenario](/operations/scenarios/deposit-escalation), built for transaction and user management rather
than for engineering.

<Warning>
  **It is deliberately not a deposit list.** There is no deposit table here, and a deposit reference is
  never resolved against one. The screen shows the **work** and the **signals** about deposits, and
  says so in a line under the title.

  It carries **no row checkbox** and **no link into the source system** — both would promise an
  operation on a handle this product cannot act on.
</Warning>

## What is on it

Seven deposit references, thirteen signals — every deposit-subject event in the sample set.

| Column | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| **Ref** | The opaque deposit reference. Mono, because it is an exact value you may need to paste |
| **Signal** | What was said about it, and by whom |
| **State** | **The one coloured field on the screen** |
| **Result** | What it produced — a task, a case signal, or nothing |

<Note>
  **One strong colour signal per row**, and here it is `state`. Everything else stays quiet. That is
  the same restraint rule the whole product follows, applied to the field actually worth scanning.
</Note>

## Absences, each with its reason

Five cells on this screen are empty on purpose, and each says why rather than showing a zero or a
dash alone:

- Two deposits have **no amount** — the publisher did not send one
- One has **no sender**
- One case has **no status**
- One task has **no deposit** attached

<Warning>
  **An absence is not a zero.** *"No amount was sent"* is a publisher gap. *"The amount is zero"* is a
  fact about money. Rendering the first as the second would be a false statement about a payment.
</Warning>

## One conflict left visible

<Warning>
  **The sample data and the measured catalogue disagree about what these events are called.**

  The fixtures say `treasury.deposit.attributed` and `treasury.deposit.attribution_failed`.

  The catalogue measured from the source service says `customer.deposit.received` and
  `customer.deposit.unattributed`.

  Both are on the canvas. **Neither was quietly picked.**
</Warning>

Why that matters to you rather than only to engineering: an event type is also the thing a workflow
matches on. Two names for one occurrence is precisely how a configured reaction silently stops
firing — and this one is caught while it is still a document disagreement rather than a production
one.

## What you actually do here

Nothing, on this screen. It is a **reading** surface.

The work it points at lives in [My tasks](/operations/my-tasks) and
[Cases](/operations/cases); anything that needs a person right now surfaces on
[Attention](/operations/attention).
