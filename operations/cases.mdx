## What a case is

A **case** is a long-running sequence of steps — onboarding a customer, an enhanced due-diligence
review, assembling and filing a report.

Some steps are automatic. Some are yours. Some are just waiting.

| Step kind | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Automatic** | The platform does it — calls a vendor, generates a document, writes a projection |
| **Human** | It becomes a task with an owner and a deadline |
| **Wait** | It is waiting for something external, with a timer armed |
| **Branch** | It goes one way or another based on an earlier outcome |
| **Parallel** | Several things run at once and join afterwards |

## Why this screen currently shows no status at all

<Warning>
  **No status pill is drawn on any row — deliberately.**

  A neutral pill would read as `waiting`, which is **a real status value**. Drawing one where no status
  exists would state something false about a live case.
</Warning>

The same discipline runs through the rest: position, step list, timers, the pinned version and the
definition name are all rendered as **absences with their reason**, because the underlying case data
does not exist in the sample set yet.

**Even the default filter is drawn as unevaluable.** *Open* means a status in running, waiting or
escalated — and no row carries a status, so the list is shown unfiltered and the footer says so
rather than silently showing everything.

<Note>
  **One case's scope is provably broken, and it is marked.** The rule deriving who can see it points
  at a field no deposit event actually carries, so the derivation produces nothing.

  The word **unscoped is deliberately not used** — *unscoped* is a positive claim that something is
  visible to everyone, and here emptiness has not been established. It is marked as a **condition**,
  not a status.
</Note>

The case detail answers one question that used to take four systems: **where is this, and why is it
stuck?**

<Steps>
  <Step title="The ordered steps">
    Every step, in order, with its kind and its state. Completed, running, waiting, failed, skipped.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What it is waiting on right now">
    Named explicitly. In the example above: a source-of-funds document, past its 7-day deadline —
    which is why it escalated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Armed timers">
    What will happen if nothing changes, and when. A reminder, an escalation, a hard deadline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sub-tasks">
    The human steps, handed to real people, with their own owners and deadlines.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The pinned definition version">
    Which version of the process this case is running. See below — this matters more than it looks.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Cases are pinned to a version

When a case starts, it is **pinned to the process definition version in force at that moment**. If
somebody edits the definition tomorrow, cases already running keep the old one.

<Note>
  **Why this matters to you:** a case that started under last quarter's rules is completed under last
  quarter's rules. Nobody's in-flight work changes shape underneath them.

  **Why it matters to compliance:** a regulated case is auditable against the rules that were
  actually in force when it started — which is the only version an auditor cares about.
</Note>

Old versions are never deleted. That is a real storage cost, accepted deliberately.

## Why a case is stuck — the honest answers

| The screen says | It means | Who fixes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waiting on a human step | A person has it and has not finished | Them, or their lead |
| Waiting on a human step, **past deadline** | It escalated | Whoever the escalation named |
| Waiting on a provider callback | A vendor was asked and has not answered | A timer will escalate; nothing to do yet |
| A step **failed** | A vendor call exhausted its retries | An engineer or an admin |
| Waiting with **no future timer** | Something went wrong in configuration | A periodic guard catches this — it is not left to be noticed |

<Warning>
  **"Waiting with no future timer" is checked for on purpose.** A case waiting on something that will
  never arrive, with no armed timer, is invisible by construction — it never fails, so nothing alerts.
  A periodic guard looks for exactly that state.
</Warning>

## There is no undo

This is deliberate and worth knowing before you look for the button.

<Note>
  **Compensation after a human has been told something is fiction.**

  There is no automatic rollback and no reverse-execution engine. Where a step needs to be undone, it
  is modelled as an **explicit compensating step** — visible in the case like any other step, with an
  owner and a record.

  An automatic "undo" that sends a second message reversing the first is not a rollback. It is two
  events, and pretending otherwise makes the history harder to read, not easier.
</Note>

## What a case is not

- **Not a BPMN diagram.** Sequence, branch, parallel, wait and timer. Nothing more. If a flow needs
  more than that, it belongs in a dedicated engine — a decision taken deliberately rather than by
  accumulation.
- **Not a ticket.** A ticket has a status; a case has a *position* in a defined sequence, a version,
  and armed timers.
- **Not where your work lives.** Your work is a **task**. The case is the thing your task is a step
  of.
