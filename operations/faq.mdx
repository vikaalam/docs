<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I still get chat notifications?" icon="comment">
    Yes, if that is how your channel policy is configured. Chat remains a perfectly good
    **destination**.

    What changes is that chat is no longer where the **work** lives. If you miss a message, delete it,
    or are on leave, the task is still there with its deadline still running.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if I'm on leave and work is assigned to me?" icon="plane">
    The SLA timer fires and the work **escalates to the fallback in the responsibility chain**. It
    does not sit unread until you return.

    If you are deactivated upstream in the company directory, the person projection picks that up and
    you stop receiving new work with no manual step from anyone.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I reassign a task to someone else?" icon="user-pen">
    Yes, subject to your permissions — and the reassignment is recorded on the task's activity with
    who did it and when.

    The one exception is a four-eyes step: the engine excludes specific people by design, and that
    exclusion is not something a reassignment can route around. An attempt is recorded as a **rejected
    action**, not silently ignored.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why can't I see another team's queue?" icon="lock">
    A queue is the main **permission scope**. You hold permissions inside your queues and not inside
    others'.

    This is enforced on the server, not by hiding buttons — the interface mirrors your permissions so
    navigation makes sense, but a hidden control is never treated as a permission by itself.

    If you genuinely need access, that is a grant, and grants are authored in the company's central
    auth service — not in this product.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Something is in Attention that isn't my job. What do I do?" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Attention shows everything that needs *a* person, not everything that needs *you*. Three of the
    common rows belong elsewhere:

    | Row | Belongs to |
    | --- | --- |
    | `unmapped` — nobody configured a reaction | A configurator |
    | `dead` — an automated step exhausted its retries | An engineer or an admin |
    | `no_recipient` — the rules resolved to nobody | Whoever owns the responsibility rules for that domain |

    Every row navigates to the screen that owns it, and that screen has the right context and the
    right people.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A deadline is wrong for this kind of work. Can it be changed?" icon="clock">
    Yes, and it does not need a deploy. SLAs live on the **task type**, which is configuration.

    Changing it produces an audit entry with the editor's name and a before/after diff — because
    changing a compliance deadline is a consequential act, not a preference.

    Existing tasks keep the deadline they were created with. New ones get the new one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I undo something?" icon="rotate-left">
    Not automatically, and that is deliberate.

    **Compensation after a human has been told something is fiction.** There is no reverse-execution
    engine. Where a step needs to be undone, it is modelled as an **explicit compensating step** —
    visible in the case like any other step, with an owner and a record.

    An automatic "undo" that sends a second message reversing the first is not a rollback; it is two
    events, and pretending otherwise makes the history harder to read.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why is a notification amber instead of red?" icon="circle-half-stroke">
    Amber means the action **completed** and reached **nobody**.

    Red would send you looking for an outage. Green would be a lie. Amber says the thing you actually
    need to know, and it usually points at a gap in the responsibility rules rather than a broken
    integration.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The same event seems to have happened twice. Which one is real?" icon="clone">
    Both are real arrivals; only one produced work.

    Events are de-duplicated by an occurrence id. A redelivery is marked `duplicate` and dropped, so
    **a fact becomes work exactly once** however many times it was delivered.

    If you see two rows with **different** occurrence ids, they are two genuinely different
    occurrences — for example, an approve → reject → approve sequence on one record is three events,
    not one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why does a case say it's waiting when nothing is happening?" icon="hourglass">
    That is the honest state. A case can be waiting on a person, on a customer document, or on a
    vendor callback — and the case names which.

    Every wait has a **timer armed**: a reminder, then an escalation, then a hard deadline. Nothing
    waits indefinitely in silence.

    There is even a periodic guard looking for the pathological case — *waiting with no future timer*
    — because a case waiting on something that will never arrive never fails, so nothing would alert.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who can see the work I did?" icon="eye">
    Anyone with permission on that queue, plus anyone with audit read access.

    Your actions are recorded permanently with your name, the time, and the before/after state — not
    because you remembered to note it, but because the record is written inside the same transaction
    as the change.

    **This applies to denied attempts too.** A permission you did not have, or a four-eyes rule you
    tripped, is itself recorded.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When can I actually use this?" icon="calendar">
    There is no running system today — this is a design.

    Operations feels the change most at **Phase 2**, when human work gets an owner, an SLA and a
    history. Phase 1 comes first and replaces the scattered chat calls; Phase 0 before it collects
    events without reacting to them, so you will not see anything during it.

    See the [roadmap](/overview/roadmap) for what each phase makes visible.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
