The fullest compliance scenario in the design: a human determination made *inside* the platform
becomes a derived fact, a statutory clock starts, a case assembles evidence, drafts a narrative,
passes quality control under a four-eyes rule, renders a reproducible artifact, files it through the
single outbound door — **and is not "done" until a receipt comes back.**

## Today, without the platform

- The determination lives in the monitoring tool's own case notes. **Nothing outside that tool knows
  a clock has started.**
- The report is built by hand: four systems, a spreadsheet, a narrative pasted into a template, an
  export.
- The numbers are whatever those systems returned **that afternoon**. Re-running next year returns
  different numbers, and nobody can say whether that is a correction or an error.
- The file is uploaded to the regulator's portal by whoever has the credentials. The proof of filing
  is **a screenshot in a shared drive**.
- Quality control is "someone senior looked at it", evidenced by a chat message.
- If the regulator rejects it, the rejection email lands in **one person's inbox**.

<Warning>
  The distinctive failure here is not lateness. It is that **"we filed it" is a claim rather than a
  record** — and a claim does not survive an audit three years later.
</Warning>

## With the platform

<Steps>
  <Step title="An analyst makes a determination" icon="user-check">
    Inside the platform, as the outcome of a review task. The outcome is structured, and it carries
    the analyst's identity and reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The determination becomes a derived fact" icon="arrows-turn-right">
    The workflow publishes a new event — a *derived* one, carrying the causation of the human decision
    that produced it.

    From here it is indistinguishable from an external fact. **The loop is uniform**, which is why
    everything downstream works the same way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Three workflows match, and all three run" icon="filter">
    | Workflow | Does |
    | --- | --- |
    | Record the determination | Writes a typed, retained projection — the record-keeping obligation |
    | Open the case | Starts the multi-step filing process |
    | Start the clock | Notifies, with the statutory deadline attached |

    Concerns attach to one fact independently. None of the three knows the others exist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The case assembles evidence" icon="folder-open">
    Automatic steps fetch documents and run screening — through **logical capabilities**, not named
    vendors.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A human drafts the narrative" icon="pen">
    A task with an owner, a deadline, and a form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A second human performs quality control" icon="user-check">
    Under a **four-eyes** control: the drafter may not be the approver, the engine enforces it, and an
    attempt to be both is recorded as a rejected action.

    This replaces "someone senior looked at it, evidenced by a chat message".
  </Step>
  <Step title="A reproducible artifact is rendered" icon="file-invoice">
    From the reporting module's **own projection** of the facts — not by re-reading four systems this
    afternoon.

    The run carries a period, a watermark, a row count, a checksum, and a duration.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It is submitted to a named destination" icon="paper-plane">
    `regulator-str-filing` — a configured row, not a URL. Through the single outbound door, which is
    the only component holding credentials.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The receipt comes back, and is stored as evidence" icon="receipt">
    **The run is not done until it does.**

    The receipt itself is stored — not a pointer to the invocation that produced it, because the
    invocation is retained for months and the filing for years.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The statutory clock

A filing window is a real deadline with a real consequence, and it is modelled as one.

<Warning>
  **Two related open questions are recorded rather than assumed, and both matter here.**

  **A business-day calendar.** SLAs are stated in calendar durations, but a filing window is counted
  in **business days in the regulator's jurisdiction**. Nothing in the architecture owns a calendar,
  and every module that computes a deadline would need the same one.

  **Absolute deadlines.** Timers are relative durations anchored at step entry. A statutory window is
  anchored to an **external date**. That needs an instance-level deadline with an anchor, an offset,
  and a calendar.

  Neither is solved. Both are named, because a filing deadline computed in calendar days when the
  statute says business days is wrong in exactly the direction that matters.
</Warning>

## What survives afterwards

Three years later, a reviewer can answer every one of these from the platform:

| Question | Answered by |
| --- | --- |
| Who made the determination, and when? | The task outcome, and its audit entry |
| Under what rules was the case run? | The **pinned definition version** |
| Who drafted, and who approved? | Two task outcomes, two actors — and the four-eyes control that separated them |
| What data went into the filing? | The run's period, watermark, and row count |
| Would re-running produce the same file? | The **checksum** |
| Where was it sent? | The named destination |
| Did they accept it? | The **stored receipt** |
| Was anything altered since? | The tamper-evidence seals |
| Who has read or exported this since? | Audit entries — reads of the record are recorded too |
