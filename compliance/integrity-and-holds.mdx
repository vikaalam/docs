## Tamper-evidence, honestly described

An hourly job computes a cryptographic checkpoint over each closed interval of audit entries and
stores it. Verification recomputes the checkpoint and compares.

<Note>
  **This screen is a verification surface, and its substance is a sentence rather than a table.**

  What a passing check proves — and what it leaves open — matters more than the list of intervals.
  That is why the claim sits at the top of the screen and the seal table sits underneath it.
</Note>

## What a passing check proves

| Proven | Not proven |
| --- | --- |
| The sealed entries have not been altered since the seal was written | That every change *produced* an entry |
| The chain of seals is unbroken over the verified range | That entries after the last seal are protected — that is the **unsealed tail** |
| The entries are in the order they were committed | That the content of an entry is *correct* — only that it is unchanged |

<Warning>
  **A detection window exists, and it is the seal interval.** A change made and reverted inside one
  unsealed interval leaves the same evidence as no change at all.

  This is a property of periodic sealing, and it is stated rather than glossed. The alternative — a
  per-row hash chain — was rejected because it serialises every concurrent write for a property the
  hourly seal already provides.
</Warning>

## Never verified is not a failure

A range that has never been checked is rendered as **neutral**, not as an error.

<Note>
  A check that has not run is not a failed check. Colouring it red trains people to ignore red.
</Note>

There is also **no repair action** on this screen, and its absence is a specification rather than an
omission. A tamper-evidence surface that offers to fix a discrepancy is a surface that can be used to
hide one.

## Legal holds

<img
  src="/images/screens/legal-holds.webp"
  alt="Legal holds — what is held, by whom, and since when"
  noZoom
/>

A **legal hold** blocks destruction for a scope. While it is in force:

- An erasure request for anything within its scope is **refused**
- The refusal is **itself audited** — it is a record, not a silent no-op
- Artifact retention cannot expire the held items — **the hold wins** over the retention date

## Active means one thing

The state of a hold is expressed by the absence of a release date. A hold with no release date is in
force; a hold with one is not.

<Note>
  **The absence *is* the datum.** Both hold states render neutral — nothing here is painted green,
  amber, or accented for a hold being in force. A hold is a fact, not an alarm.
</Note>

## The interaction with erasure

Holds and erasure are shown together because they interact, and because either one read alone gives
the wrong impression.

<Steps>
  <Step title="An erasure request arrives">
    For a subject, or for a scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The platform checks for a hold">
    If one is in force, the request is **rejected** and the rejection is recorded with its reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If no hold applies, the key is destroyed">
    Not the record. The record survives, stays countable, and remains part of the history — only the
    key that made its personal data readable is destroyed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The destruction is audited">
    So that the erasure can be **proven**, which is the half of an erasure obligation that a deletion
    cannot satisfy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **One finding is rendered on the design itself, rather than hidden.**

  The interlock currently queries holds **by subject**. A hold scoped by *correlation id* — the scope
  that a case-level hold naturally takes — names no subject, so **the one hold in the worked corpus
  would not be found by that query.**

  Recorded as a defect to close, not designed around.
</Warning>

## What is not yet decided

These are recorded as open questions rather than answered with a plausible default.

| Open | Why it is open |
| --- | --- |
| How many entries and artifacts a hold actually covers | There is no count anywhere yet — and **both confirmation dialogues depend on it**, because a consequential action needs a measured blast radius, not an invented one |
| Artifact retention duration | A regulatory answer, not an engineering one |
| Whether an audit export should require a stated reason | A compliance decision that has not been taken |
| Field-level redaction for identity-verification responses | Vendor responses contain identity documents. The redaction policy and its retention **must be agreed with compliance before that module ships**, not after |

<Note>
  Where a number was never stated, that is recorded as an open question rather than filled in with a
  default. A default retention that nobody chose is worse than a blank, because it looks decided.
</Note>
