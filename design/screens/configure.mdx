Everything that changes what the platform does — owned by `process`, `notification`, `integration`
and `reporting`.

<Note>
  **Every screen in this group embeds its own audit history.** Who changed this, when, and what it
  looked like before — on the same screen as the thing being changed.

  That is the design paying back the cost of configuration living in a database rather than in git.
</Note>

## Process definitions

<img src="/images/screens/process-definitions.webp" alt="Process definitions — which flows exist, and what runs on which version" noZoom />

`process` — which flows exist and what is running on which version.

Both rows here are **derived**: a definition is on screen because a workflow starts or signals it, and
the running counts are the instance references those executions returned.

<Note>
  Published and draft render as **absent** — no fixture carries a version for any definition, and the
  screen says so rather than showing an illustrative number.
</Note>

## Channel policies

<img src="/images/screens/channel-policies.webp" alt="Channel policies — category by severity, mandatory channels, quiet hours" noZoom />

`notification` — category × severity → channels, plus mandatory channels, quiet hours and the digest
window.

<Warning>
  The four categories are a **closed union** — incident · assignment · approval · audit — deliberately
  **not** domain-flavoured.

  A conflict between that union and domain-flavoured values in the sample data is **filed as an open
  finding and left open**, with the sample named as the likely defect. Severity is the column axis and
  is **still never a pill**.
</Warning>

## Destinations

<img src="/images/screens/destinations.webp" alt="Destinations — named rows, their resolution state, and their references" noZoom />

`integration`, under the **global-resource treatment**. Owns every outbound URL in the product.

<Warning>
  A free-form URL is forbidden **everywhere else**. This screen is the single deliberate exception the
  rule was written around.
</Warning>

Three of the destinations drawn are referenced by workflow actions; three regulator codes are
referenced by nothing — which exercises the **unreferenced** state, a neutral pill and never a
warning. An unused destination is not a problem; an unknown one is.

<Note>
  Only one destination has a base URL, paths, auth and a timeout anywhere in the sample set. The other
  five render *"not in the seed set"* rather than an invented hostname.

  The resolution line is **a check, not a promise** — egress resolves again at call time.
</Note>

## Report definitions

<img src="/images/screens/report-definitions.webp" alt="Report definitions — versioned, each with a named destination" noZoom />

`reporting` — versioned, and **a run pins a version**.

Destinations are **named rows**: `regulator-str-filing`, `fintrac-lvctr` — not an endpoint.

Read-only for a compliance reader; the owner edits.

## Responsibility, people and teams

`directory` owns these, and they are the answer to the one question no service in the audited estate
could answer: **who is responsible for a thing.**

<Note>
  Rules are **ordered, and the first match wins** — and they key on **what kind of work it is**, never
  on what kind of thing it is about.

  Keying on the subject is the trap that only shows up in production: an item matching two rules lands
  in two queues and two teams work it.
</Note>

## Task types

`task` owns the shape of each kind of human work: its form, its SLA, its responsibility descriptor,
and whether it is an **approval**.

<Note>
  That last flag exists so that a control that **stopped because nobody is eligible** can be told from
  a queue that is merely quiet. They share a backend condition and **must not share a rendering**.
</Note>
