## The policy is not authored here

<Warning>
  **This platform is a Policy Decision and Enforcement Point. It is never a Policy Administration
  Point.**

  There is no role editor and no grant screen, and their absence is **correct**.
</Warning>

| Owned by the auth service | Owned here |
| --- | --- |
| Resource and action vocabulary; which role holds which permission; which person holds which role | Both guards, evaluated in-process |
| The policy rows loaded into the local policy store | **Scope evaluation** — the labels are derived from the work's own columns, which no external service can read |
| Grant audit, access review, expiry | The **route-prefix module guard**, which is a property of this platform's routing, not a fact about a principal |

### Why the policy moved out

<Note>
  The estate runs **seven distinct inbound auth mechanisms** and no permission is portable between
  them. One human's authority is re-declared in every service, and a revocation is *N* manual edits
  nobody can enumerate.

  `workspace-ops` would have added an **eighth**.
</Note>

Three alternatives, and why each failed:

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A central authorize-per-request call | Makes another service the availability and p99 floor of every request here — and **it cannot see the scope columns**, so the third dimension dies |
| An auth module inside this platform | Same coupling, and it puts the operational platform in every other service's login path |
| The identity provider's own authorization services | Same blind spot, and policy then lives in a vendor console where it cannot be screened or approval-gated |

### The driver is not written here either

Snapshot loading, the watcher subscription, and the enforcer come from the estate's shared client
package. This platform's adapter binds it to the two guards and to the scope columns.

<Note>
  A hand-rolled copy would be a **sixth implementation** of a security control the estate has already
  demonstrated it cannot keep consistent.
</Note>

## Two consequences, accepted deliberately

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Revocation is eventually consistent" icon="clock">
    Bounded to a few seconds at p99.

    For the three global resources, that window is the residual risk — and the control for it is a
    **short-lived token**, not a faster policy channel.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Boot behaviour is asymmetric on purpose" icon="power-off">
    The platform **must still start** when the policy service is unreachable *after* boot, serving its
    last known snapshot and alerting.

    It must **refuse to start** when no snapshot is available *at* boot — because a process that
    starts with no policy denies every request, which is **an outage wearing the costume of a bug**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The frontend mirrors, it does not enforce

<Warning>
  **The server remains the only enforcement point. A hidden button is not a permission.**
</Warning>

Route guards mirror the two layers client-side **for navigation only**, and the bundle is split per
module so an operator with `task` access never downloads the `integration` credential screens.

The token lifecycle that follows from being a single-page application — PKCE, in-memory access token,
cookie-borne refresh, the single-flight renewal guard — is stated once in
[Reference → Authentication](/reference/authentication).

## The struck row

Authorization policy used to appear in the list of things that are configuration editable in the
interface. It was struck — and the strike **closed a gap rather than opening one**.

<Note>
  Every other row in that list has a screen. This one never did: the navigation has no role or
  permission editor and never had one.

  A configuration row with no screen is normally a defect. Here it was **the architecture pointing at
  a service that did not exist yet.** It exists now, and the absence of the screen is correct.
</Note>
