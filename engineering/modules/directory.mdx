> **People, teams, and who is responsible for what.** A projection of the company identity provider,
> plus responsibility rules that belong to us and are never overwritten.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Schema | `directory` |
| Layer | Foundation |
| Depends on | *nothing — it is the bottom of the graph* |
| Depended on by | `task`, `notification`, `automation` (the `project` write path), the interface. **Not `process`** — it escalates through `notification`, which resolves against the same chain |
| Slice | **0 — Foundation** |
| Extraction trigger | On-call scheduling or a rule engine arrives |

## Why it is its own module

Two questions collapse into one here, and that is the point:

| Question | Nature | Owned by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Who is this person?* | Pure projection — name, email, team, still employed | **Upstream** |
| *Who is responsible for X?* | Pure policy — including whether we are willing to route work to them at all | **Us** |

<Note>
  They are always read together to answer *"who"*, which is why separating them into two modules was
  rejected. One module, one answer.
</Note>

## The gap this fills

<Warning>
  **Not one of the seven services in the audited estate models who is responsible for a thing.** Every
  destination is a channel, chosen by the code that happens to be running.

  That is the gap this module fills, and it is why responsibility is a **module** rather than a
  column.
</Warning>

## The upstream is the auth service, not the identity provider directly

The estate's auth service is the Policy Administration Point, and it performs the scheduled identity
sweep on this module's behalf.

<Note>
  Everything that says *"the identity provider"* means *"the identity provider as projected by the
  auth service"*. The distinction matters for two reasons:

  1. This module needs **no identity-provider client of its own**.
  2. **Roles and grants are not projected here at all.** This module answers *who a person is* and
     *who is responsible for a subject* — never *what they are permitted to do*.

  Responsibility roles are **operational routing**, not authorization.
</Note>

## Two activity flags, not one

A person carries **both**:

| Flag | Owned by | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upstream active | The identity provider | Still in the company directory |
| Locally enabled | Us | We have not suspended them |

Effective activity is the **conjunction**.

<Note>
  One column with precedence rules was rejected — unreadable, and it loses the distinction between
  *"suspended by us, still employed"* and *"gone from the identity provider"*, which need different
  escalation wording.
</Note>

## The sweep is the population mechanism, not just drift correction

<Warning>
  This module ships in **slice 0**, but `automation` does not exist until slice 1 — and the projection
  feeds through `automation`.

  An events-only design therefore ships a foundation **with no people in it**, where every
  responsibility lookup correctly returns empty and nothing works.
</Warning>

So the daily reconciliation sweep is promoted from *drift correction* to **the population
mechanism**, and drift correction thereafter. That is also more robust — it makes the event path a
freshness optimisation rather than the only way data arrives.

## The fail-open trap

<Warning>
  The estate's current team-membership call **fails open to an empty set**.

  A projection must therefore treat an empty response as a **failure**, never as a membership change.
  Treating it as data silently removes everyone from every team, and surfaces weeks later as
  unassigned work.
</Warning>

## Responsibility rules

Rules are **ordered, and the first match wins.**

```
rule A   domain = compliance · role = reviewer   → team T-COMPLIANCE   priority 10
rule B   domain = operations · role = reviewer   → team T-OPERATIONS   priority 20
```

<Warning>
  **Responsibility keys on what kind of work it is, never on what kind of thing it is about.**

  Keying on the subject type is the trap that only shows up in production: a deposit that both failed
  attribution *and* breached a threshold matches both rules, lands in both queues, and two teams work
  it.

  A deposit is not owned by a department. *Reviewing a deposit's name* is.
</Warning>

The mechanism is a responsibility descriptor on the **task type** — `{domain, role}` — which `task`
passes to this module. A rule written against a task type is deliberately **not representable**.

<Warning>
  **No relationship line leaves this schema, and that is the boundary rule rather than an omission.** The 4 lines above are real foreign keys inside `directory`.

  Bare cross-schema references — drawn nowhere, because the database does not enforce them: A person reference into this module's own `persons` table is a real key; anything naming a subject is not.
</Warning>

<Note>
  **This module publishes, so it owns an outbox** — and in an earlier version of the roster it did
  not. That meant its responsibility-change event was either lost on a crash or written inside another
  module's transaction.

  The publishes-therefore-outbox rule is derivable and checkable, which is how the gap was found.
</Note>

## Open question carried here

<Warning>
  **A business-day calendar.** SLAs and statutory deadlines are stated in calendar durations, but a
  filing window is counted in business days in the regulator's jurisdiction.

  The tables exist here; **the semantics do not**, and every module that computes a deadline would
  need the same calendar. Recorded rather than assumed.
</Warning>
