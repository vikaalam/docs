## Still open

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="2 · Personal data in vendor invocation records" icon="id-card">
    Identity-verification responses contain identity documents.

    **The field-level redaction policy and its retention must be agreed with compliance before slice
    3, not after.** This is a hard sequencing requirement, not a preference.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="3 · Artifact retention and legal hold duration" icon="box-archive">
    **A regulatory answer, not an engineering one.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="4 · Notifier edition limits" icon="plug">
    Self-hosted notification platforms often gate team seats and environments in their community
    edition.

    Whether that is acceptable is a **commercial** decision. It gates the notification design detail,
    not the architecture — and it is also the reason the frontend framework must never have been
    chosen from that vendor's component catalogue.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="5 · Chat destination during the strangler period" icon="comment">
    Whether parallel-run messages go to the **same channel, marked**, or to a **shadow channel**
    changes how the comparison is performed in slice 1.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="6 · Ingest-history retention" icon="clock">
    Not only a storage number. It is the **backfill horizon** for every report fact type declared
    later — shortening it shortens what can ever be reconstructed.

    Raw envelopes may also hold data that never passed a redaction policy.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="7 · A business-day calendar" icon="calendar">
    SLAs and statutory deadlines are stated in calendar durations, but a filing window is counted in
    **business days in the regulator's jurisdiction**.

    Nothing in the architecture owns a calendar, and every module that computes a deadline would need
    the same one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="8 / 10 · Absolute deadlines" icon="hourglass">
    Timers are **relative durations anchored at step entry**. A statutory window is anchored to an
    **external date**.

    This needs an instance-level deadline with an **anchor, an offset, and a calendar** — **before
    slice 4, or filings are timed from the wrong moment.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="13 · Inbox read state is not measurable from here" icon="eye">
    The in-app inbox belongs to the notifier driver.

    *"How long before anyone looked?"* — **the number that decides queue staffing** — cannot be
    answered from this platform's data without copying state the notifier owns and that would go
    stale.

    Three ways out: the notifier exposes it as a queryable fact; a read-receipt event flows back into
    the stream; or the question permanently belongs to the notifier's own reporting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="14 · No report fact type spans a whole case" icon="chart-line">
    Two sanctioned projections cover a **single unit** each — a case's outcome and duration, and who
    completed what and when.

    **What is missing is the cross-module figure:** elapsed time across a case's process steps *and*
    the task work inside them, which neither projection answers alone.

    <Warning>
      Whoever is first asked for that has **no sanctioned path** and will write the cross-schema JOIN
      the boundary rules forbid. **The prohibition and the missing alternative are the same finding.**
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="15 · The calendar is bound to a team, and the deadlines that matter are not a team's" icon="scale-balanced">
    A calendar on a team is right for an SLA on human work. A statutory filing window is counted in
    the **regulator's** business days — and a deployment can face **more than one regulator at once**.

    The worked profile shows **four legal entities and a filing schedule pinned to a timezone
    belonging to none of them.**

    So `process` and `reporting` need to be able to *name* a calendar, and nothing lets them.

    <Note>
      Related to items 7, 8 and 10, and **not the same as any of them**: those ask what a deadline is
      anchored *to*; this asks **whose week it is counted in**.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="16 · A provisional reference format is indistinguishable from a verified one" icon="fingerprint">
    A subject type carries a format that a reference must match, and validation happens at ingest.

    But the *"fire it once, look at the shape, then build the workflow"* loop depends on an unmapped
    event being **stored** — so an event whose subject type is registered with a **provisional format
    nobody has verified** looks exactly like one that was checked.

    **A catch-all format and a null one need to be different states, and are not.**
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Answered, kept in place

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1 · Source of person identity — answered" icon="circle-check">
    The identity provider, projected on a **scheduled sweep performed by the estate's auth service**,
    which becomes the directory's upstream in place of a direct call.

    <Warning>
      The sweep must treat an **empty response as a failure**, never as a membership change. The
      estate's current team lookup **fails open to an empty set**, which would silently remove everyone
      from every team and surface later as unassigned work.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="9 · Retention for action executions — answered 2026-07-27" icon="circle-check">
    **It inherits the ingest history's retention exactly, and is partitioned by month to make that
    sweepable.**

    It cannot be an independent number: an action execution is the **child** of an ingested event, and
    a trace reads both to answer one question. Retaining either without the other yields **half a
    trace** — an event whose consequences vanished, or an execution whose cause did.

    Partitioning follows from the retention, not from volume: a matching month partition means one
    drop removes an event and its executions **together**, and they can never diverge.

    <Warning>
      **The related trap stands unchanged:** report submissions are retained for years and point at a
      table retained for months. **The stored receipt, not the pointer, is the evidence.**
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="11 · Four eyes could not exclude the maker — answered 2026-07-28" icon="circle-check">
    **An explicit `excludeTriggerActor` flag.** The triggering event's actor is added to **the same
    exclusion set** the step codes populate — one set, one mechanism, and the same failure mode: an
    exclusion that empties the queue **escalates, never a silent self-approval**.

    It **defaults to off**, so no published definition changes meaning until someone edits it.

    A boolean is admissible here for the one reason a bare "needs four eyes" boolean is rejected
    elsewhere: there is exactly **one** trigger actor per instance, so it says nothing ambiguous. The
    two alternatives — naming the trigger in the exclusion list, or a seed-context path — are recorded
    with their costs.

    <Note>
      **This was the only item on the list that was a control that was *off*, rather than a number or a
      name that was missing.** The others cost correctness later; this one was live.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="12 · A rendered link passes none of the egress controls — ownership and storage answered" icon="circle-check">
    The outbound-URL rules stop **our server** fetching a hostile address. A link rendered into a
    reviewer's task form is **never fetched by us**, so none of them apply — while being **more likely
    to be clicked than any URL in the system**.

    **A different victim, not a weaker version of the same problem:** the target is a compliance
    officer's browser and their trust in our interface.

    **Owner: `integration`** — nothing else can hold it. The check compares a host against a
    destinations row; a module owns its own tables, so no other module may read one, and no other
    module may even import the client it would need.

    **Where it runs: ingest.** A link whose host fails verification makes the event `invalid` — a
    disposition only ingest can assign. The verdict travels with the dispatch payload and is **never
    re-derived at render time**, because the task form and the inbox entry are two renderings of one
    payload and a second derivation could disagree with the first.

    **Storage, answered 2026-07-27:** a verified-links table in `integration`, written during ingest
    validation by the same call that produces the verdict.

    <Note>
      **What the table buys is the re-check.** A link verified at ingest is a verdict about a
      destination *as it was then*. When a destination is deleted or its host changes, those rows are
      the query that finds every already-rendered link the change invalidates — impossible while the
      verdict existed only inside a payload.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="17 · Nothing marked a task type as an approval — answered 2026-07-27" icon="circle-check">
    **An `is_approval` flag on the task type, seeded per deployment.**

    An approval whose exclusion leaves **nobody eligible** is a compliance gap; an ordinary unassigned
    task is a delayed one. **They share a backend condition and must not share a rendering.**

    It is a `task` column and not a derivation, because the only other candidate — reading the process
    step's four-eyes configuration — is exactly the cross-module read the boundary rules forbid.
    **`task` must stay usable for work no process created**, so it cannot learn what a process step
    knows.

    This is **the same control as item 11 seen from the other end**: 11 asks who may be excluded; 17
    asks what happens when the exclusion empties the queue.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="18 · An invalid event stored the envelope and not the reason — answered 2026-07-27" icon="circle-check">
    **A rejection object on the ingest row** — a code, a **field**, and a detail — written only when the
    status is `invalid`.

    Structured and not free text because rejection happens on a **named field**, and *"which field"* is
    the whole value of the record; a sentence would have to be parsed back to be useful.

    <Warning>
      **This made the two doors symmetric, which is the point.** The HTTP door already returned the
      reason in its response; the stream door had nowhere to put it, so the reason reached nobody.

      That made it a defect by the contract's own test — *a behaviour that exists on only one transport
      is a defect* — and it disabled the one migration aid the whole project argues for: a team moving
      off hand-rolled sends fails first on the **seven forbidden envelope fields**, and the platform
      could not tell them which.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="19 · A responsibility rule could not be named — answered 2026-07-27" icon="circle-check">
    **A unique code on the rule**, returned alongside the id.

    The resolution result must carry a rule reference, because *"the system chose this person"* is not
    an acceptable answer in an incident review — but what came back was a UUID.

    Every surface that must show *why this person* was therefore showing an identifier **nobody can say
    out loud**, and the predicate had to be reassembled to make the answer legible.

    The gap was small and it landed on **the one question the directory module exists to answer.**
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Two concentration risks, recorded rather than solved

<Warning>
  **The outbox pump has no priority lane** — a slow provider's retries share a queue with an escalation
  notification.

  **Batch task creation fires a thundering herd of SLA timers** at the same instant.

  Both need a decision **before slice 2 carries real volume.**
</Warning>

## One thing that is not verified

<Warning>
  **The audit transaction wiring.** Enlistment behaviour when an HTTP interceptor and an ORM subscriber
  share one entity manager **differs between transaction libraries**.

  The concept is sound. **The wiring must be proven with a spike before it is relied on** — and the
  transaction-context library becomes load-bearing platform infrastructure as a result.
</Warning>

## Where these come from

<Note>
  **Every use-case walkthrough is required to end with what it could not settle**, and those gaps
  become findings rather than being filled with a plausible mechanism.

  Roughly **thirty findings** arrived that way, **three of them structural**. Three of the items above
  came specifically from **building screens against the documents and hitting a place where the schema
  could not answer** — each one a **missing column**, which is why none could be closed by a screen
  deciding something.

  **Recording the gap is the discipline. Inventing the mechanism is the expensive mistake.**
</Note>
